# Writing for LinkedIn

Turn the user's facts and perspective into a useful post for a specific professional reader. The skill works without postmoderno; a connector adds account context and scheduling, not a requirement to write.

For algorithm questions, reach diagnosis or advice about links, timing and formats, read [the dated evidence](references/algorithm.md) before answering. Separate documented mechanisms from editorial judgment and account observations. Do not invent scoring weights, reach multipliers or guarantees.

If postmoderno is connected, read [the hand-off](references/postmoderno.md) before using it, including when retrieving saved voice for a drafting task. A request to write text does not by itself require saving a production draft.

## Establish the message

Use the notes, supplied documents and available account context to identify:

- The reader: a role, problem or professional situation, rather than "everyone on LinkedIn."
- The point: what the reader should understand, reconsider or do.
- The goal: a useful discussion, a launch click, a hiring announcement, a lesson or another outcome the user actually wants.
- The facts available: decisions, examples, names, numbers, links and media.

Infer reasonable context from the request; ask only for missing details that change the post. Saved account voice, audience and guardrails override the writing defaults here.

**Never fabricate facts or first-person experience.** Do not invent customers, results, revenue, quotes, failures, emotions, timelines or "what I learned" stories. An opinion can be framed as an opinion; it must not become an experience the user never described. If an essential detail is missing, ask or mark a visible placeholder and explain it. A draft with placeholders needs resolution before scheduling.

## Choose a useful shape

Default to one complete feed post. Choose the length the idea needs; a character limit is a ceiling, not a target.

- **Announcement:** what changed, who benefits and the next step. Do not add unsupported product capabilities.
- **Practical lesson or explanation:** the decision or problem, concrete evidence or steps, and the useful implication. Do not manufacture a dramatic story arc.
- **Position or trade-off:** the claim, why the user holds it, and the conditions where it applies. Avoid pretending a contextual preference is a universal law.
- **Image-supported post:** use a real screenshot, diagram or other visual that contributes information; supply accurate alt text. Distinguish an image suggestion from an existing asset.
- **Document or video concept:** useful when the user wants it, but distinguish native LinkedIn formats from what their scheduling connection can publish. Check the hand-off before promising delivery.
- **Series:** each installment must stand alone and add something new. Do not turn a long post into an X-style reply chain.

A link belongs where it helps the reader and the user's goal. Keep the post useful without requiring a click. If the click is the goal, include the supplied link clearly; do not hide it in a first comment based on an unverified penalty claim. Without a link, ask for it or mark the missing detail rather than invent a URL.

## Draft in the user's voice

Give the requested number of drafts. When alternatives would help, vary the angle rather than producing cosmetic rewrites.

Lead with the actual point, useful detail or professional tension. Make the opening understandable before any feed truncation; the visible cutoff varies, so do not claim a fixed "see more" character count or treat clicks on it as guaranteed ranking gains.

Develop the point with facts the user supplied. Keep the relevant technical vocabulary and specificity. Professional writing does not require corporate language, a forced vulnerability story, or a motivational conclusion.

Use readable paragraphs and ordinary punctuation. Avoid generic openings, unsupported superlatives, repetitive one-line fragments, decorative emoji lists and empty morals unless they genuinely belong to the user's established voice. These are editorial choices, not proof of an AI detector or an automatic reach penalty.

End when the point is complete. A focused, sincere question can invite useful discussion; no question is required. Remove empty "Agree?", "comment YES", engagement exchanges and irrelevant tagging. Never propose automated comments, engagement pods or fake conversations as a distribution tactic.

When adapting an X post, keep its facts but reconsider the professional context and explanation. Do not impose X's 280-character limit, URL weighting or thread conventions.

## Check and present

Before showing the draft:

- Can each factual claim be traced to the user, account context or a verified source? Attribute third-party evidence rather than presenting it as the user's own result.
- Does the opening deliver the point, and does the body pay it off?
- Is there a clear reason this reader would find it useful?
- Is the voice recognizably the user's, without invented anecdotes or generic filler?
- Does the proposed format fit the intended publishing path?

Keep scheduled feed posts within the account's reported limit. postmoderno currently allows a 3,000-character body for LinkedIn. Do not apply X's shortened-URL count; the full link and line breaks are part of the text. Check the count with available tooling rather than guessing. If exact platform validation is unavailable, say so instead of presenting an estimate as authoritative.

Show clean copy separately from notes, unresolved placeholders and any media/alt-text suggestions. If there are alternatives, recommend one with a short reason tied to the user's goal. Do not include drafting instructions or character-count labels in the post itself.

## Scheduling and learning from results

Use [the postmoderno hand-off](references/postmoderno.md) when the user wants to save, post or schedule. Retrieve current account capabilities, preview the exact draft and time, show the preview, and wait for explicit confirmation before scheduling.

For reach questions, consider the audience, topic, format, timing and actual outcomes before prescribing a rewrite. A pair of posts is not a controlled experiment. Label account observations as observations; prioritize the user's objective over impressions alone. Claims about universal link penalties, first-hour cutoffs, ideal lengths or format boosts need evidence beyond a single account's result.
